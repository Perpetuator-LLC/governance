# Financial

How billable work is invoiced and how a retainer's invoices are audited.

On client work, the client's billing format and cadence win on *their* invoice; your record-keeping
discipline always applies (`core/AGENTS.md` → *Precedence of guidance*).

## Invoicing

- **One append-only invoice doc per engagement**, newest period at top — never a folder of per-week
  files, never split across files.
- Each period: a **paste-ready ≤500-char summary** (the verbatim line item) + a Work-Performed
  breakdown by category with hour estimates + decisions captured + key deliverables + closing total.
- Lead the summary with **business-owner-visible impact** (go-lives, sales-ready milestones, cost
  savings), not infra minutiae.
- **Rate & cadence are per engagement**, declared in that engagement's own config — never in shared
  governance. Rates, caps, billing days, payment rails and contractor terms are ENGAGEMENT facts and
  PERSONAL data: they live in a private layer (contractor terms with that contractor's invoice record;
  client terms in that engagement's overlay), and shared governance carries only the rule that they
  are declared per engagement.

## ⚠️ Auditing a retainer: count ELAPSED weeks, and name the dollars

A **flat weekly retainer invoiced on sub-week windows compresses cadence** — several short periods
bill as several weeks, and the compression is invisible in a list of invoices because every line
looks correct on its own. Audit it against the calendar, not the invoice count: **invoices per
ELAPSED week**, plus the **holes** between periods (a gap is as much a finding as an overlap, and
points the other way).

**State the dollar delta, never the drift.** *"Cadence drifted"* is unactionable and sounds like a
process quibble; *"N invoices across M elapsed weeks = $X"* is a number the payer and the payee can
both check, and it is the only form that can be settled.

⚠️ **Work billed twice under two names is REWORK, not a deduction.** When the same deliverable
appears under two branch or task names, the finding is a **delivery** one with a **process** fix
(naming, ticket hygiene, one work item per deliverable) — it is not evidence of overbilling and must
not be written up as a clawback. Getting this wrong converts a fixable process gap into an
accusation, which is expensive in a way the amount never justifies. Where genuine double-billing is
suspected, that is a separate finding requiring its own evidence.
